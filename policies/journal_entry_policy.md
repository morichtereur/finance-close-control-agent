# Journal Entry Policy

Owner: Group Accounting · Applies to: all reporting entities · Review cycle: annual

> Illustrative policy written for this prototype. It is modelled on common
> corporate accounting policy language but represents no real organisation.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy governs the creation, review, approval and posting of journal entries
in the group ledger. It applies to all manual and system-generated entries posted
by finance staff of any reporting entity, including entries posted during the
month-end close.

## 2. Segregation of duties

The preparer of a journal entry may not be its approver. Where an entry is
prepared and approved by the same user id, the entry is treated as a control
failure and must be reported to the Financial Controller of the entity,
irrespective of amount.

## 3. Documentation requirement

Every journal entry must carry a description that allows an independent reviewer
to understand the business event without contacting the preparer. Descriptions
consisting only of a reference number, a period label or generic text such as
"adjustment", "correction" or "per instruction" do not satisfy this requirement.

## 4. Approval thresholds

### 4.1 Standard approvals

Journal entries with an absolute value below EUR 50,000 require single-level
approval by the responsible accountant.

### 4.2 Second-level approval

Journal entries with an absolute value at or above EUR 50,000 require documented
second-level approval by the entity Financial Controller before posting. An entry
at or above this threshold that carries no recorded approver is a threshold
breach and must be escalated for review before the ledger is closed.

### 4.3 Splitting

Entries may not be split into multiple postings for the purpose of remaining
below an approval threshold. Two or more entries to the same account and cost
center, on the same posting date, that together exceed the second-level approval
threshold are reviewed as a single item.

## 5. Duplicate postings

A posting is treated as a suspected duplicate where another entry exists with the
same company code, account, absolute amount and document date. Suspected
duplicates must be investigated before the close is signed off. Confirmed
duplicates require a correcting entry referencing both original documents; the
correction itself follows the approval thresholds in section 4.

## 6. High-risk accounts

The following account groups are designated high risk because of their
susceptibility to management override or estimation bias:

- 199000 Suspense and clearing
- 289000 Restructuring provisions
- 480000 Other operating income
- 510000 Cost of sales adjustments
- 610000 Consulting and professional fees

Manual entries to a high-risk account are always reviewed by a person, regardless
of amount, and may not be auto-approved by an automated control routine.

## 7. Unusual entry characteristics

An entry is flagged as unusual where it shows one or more of the following
characteristics: a round amount at or above EUR 100,000, a posting to a
high-risk account by a user who does not normally post to that account, a
reversal posted within two business days without a documented reason, or an
entry posted in a period other than the period of the underlying document date.
Flagged entries require narrative justification retained with the entry.

## 8. Retention

Journal entries, their approvals and their supporting documentation are retained
for ten years in line with statutory record-keeping requirements.
