# Supporting Documentation Standard

Owner: Group Accounting · Applies to: all manual postings and reconciliations

> Illustrative standard written for this prototype.

## 1. Purpose

Supporting documentation is the evidence that a recorded transaction reflects a
real business event. This standard defines what constitutes acceptable support
and what happens when it is absent.

## 2. Acceptable supporting documentation

Acceptable support is a document, retained and retrievable, that independently
evidences the amount, the period and the business rationale of the entry.
Examples are a supplier invoice, a signed contract, a bank statement, a payroll
report, a sub-ledger extract, an approved accrual calculation, or a written
instruction from an authorised person.

A reference to another journal entry, a spreadsheet without a documented source,
or an email that states only the amount to be posted are not acceptable support.

## 3. Attachment requirement

The support must be attached to the entry or referenced by a stable document
identifier that resolves to the retained document. An entry whose document
reference does not resolve is treated as unsupported.

## 4. Missing documentation

### 4.1 Standard treatment

Where supporting documentation is missing, the preparer is requested to provide
it within two working days. The item remains an open exception until support is
provided or the entry is reversed.

### 4.2 Escalation

Where supporting documentation is missing and the entry is at or above the
second-level approval threshold, is posted to a high-risk account, or is not
provided within two working days, the item is escalated to the entity Financial
Controller. Missing documentation is never resolved by an automated system on the
basis of plausibility.

### 4.3 Documentation obtained after posting

Support obtained after posting is acceptable provided it is dated on or before
the document date of the entry and is retained with the entry. Documentation
created after the fact solely to satisfy this standard is not acceptable support
and is reported as a control failure.

## 5. Reconciliation support

Reconciling items require the same standard of support as journal entries. An
explanation without an underlying document is not support.

## 6. Language and legibility

Support in a language other than English or the entity's local language must be
accompanied by a summary translation of the amount, the counterparty, the date
and the business purpose.

## 7. Automated handling

An automated control assistant may check whether a document reference exists and
whether it resolves. It may not conclude that documentation exists on the basis
of the entry description, and it may not close an item for missing documentation.
